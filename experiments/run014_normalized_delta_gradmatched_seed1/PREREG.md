# Run014 preregistration — gradient-scale-matched normalised residual-delta control

**Experiment ID:** `run014_normalized_delta_gradmatched_seed1`
**Status:** PREREGISTERED. Written and committed before any calibration gradient was measured
and before any Run014 training step.
**Method version:** `run014-gradcal-1.0`

## 1. Research question

Run011 (normalised residual-delta pure hidden-state KD, seed 1) finished at held-out validation
loss **10.822**, far worse than Run008 (raw residual-delta, seed 1) at **7.41131**. That comparison
does not separate two explanations:

1. residual-update **magnitude** carries information the student uses; or
2. the raw and normalised objectives simply produce different **gradient scales** and therefore
   different effective optimisation strength.

> **Does normalised residual-delta KD remain substantially worse than raw residual-delta KD after
> matching its initial trainable-parameter gradient scale to the raw objective?**

This is a confirmatory mechanistic control.

## 6. Gradient-norm definition

`g` = global L2 norm of `.grad` over parameters with `requires_grad=True` only (the LoRA
parameters), **pre-clip**, with `loss_scale = 1.0`, from one forward + chunked hidden loss +
propagation, built exactly as `trainer.train()` does (bf16 autocast around forward and chunked
loss; propagation outside it; `use_reentrant=False` checkpointing; `model.train()`).

For each frozen index `i`, from the **identical seed-1 QLoRA initialisation**:
- `g_raw_i`  — normalise = False
- `g_norm_i` — normalise = True
- `ratio_i = g_raw_i / g_norm_i`

Identical initialisation is guaranteed by one model instance whose trainable-state SHA-256 digest
is checked before every measurement and at the end; gradients are zeroed between measurements.
No optimiser and no scheduler are constructed; no parameter is updated.

## 7. Multiplier estimator (frozen)

**`m = statistics.median(ratio_0, ratio_144, ratio_288, ratio_432)`** — for four values, the mean of
the 2nd and 3rd smallest ratios. `m` must be finite and positive, else Run014 is refused.

Prohibited: coefficient sweeps; alternative multipliers; adaptive or per-step rescaling;
recalibration; any post-hoc adjustment of `m` after its value is observed.

## 8. How `m` is applied

`behavioral_loss_chunked` multiplies each chunk loss by `loss_scale` immediately before its
`backward`; its returned total is built from detached floats. `m` therefore rides in
`loss_scale` (`gradient_calibration.scaled_hidden_loss`), scaling the hidden gradient **exactly
once**. Expected invocations: **128** (once per optimiser step; validation is plain CE under
`no_grad` and never calls it).

Logged `loss` / `layer_kd_loss` remain the **unweighted** normalised residual-delta loss,
comparable to Run011. The optimised objective is `m ×` that value.

## 9. Known theoretical consideration (recorded before calibration)

The training loop applies `clip_grad_norm_(trainable, 1.0)` every step, then `torch.optim.AdamW`
(default ε = 1e-8, weight decay 0). bf16 disables `GradScaler`.

AdamW's update is approximately invariant to a **constant** rescaling of the gradient (m̂/√v̂
cancels it) except through ε. A constant multiplier can therefore act mainly through the
**clip nonlinearity**: it moves the step-by-step clip factor `min(1, 1/‖g‖)`, which feeds Adam a
non-stationary scale. Consequently:

- Run014 tests the clip-mediated gradient-scale mechanism specifically, not "gradient scale"
  in an SGD sense.
- A result near Run011 is partly expected a priori and is weaker evidence than it appears: it
  rules out a clip-mediated scalar-scale explanation, not every optimisation-dynamics explanation.
- Calibration records, per sample, whether `g_raw` and `g_norm` exceed the clip threshold 1.0. These
  are diagnostics only and do not enter `m`.

This consideration does not alter the procedure or the objective.

## 11. Interpretation rules (frozen before results)

Let `F = (Run011 − Run014) / (Run011 − Run008)` = fraction of the 3.41069 gap recovered.
Noise floor `N = 0.213` (the Run010-vs-Run008 spread preregistered for Run011).

| observed | classification | statement |
|---|---|---|
| `F < 0.25` | NORMALISATION_PENALTY_NOT_EXPLAINED_BY_SCALE | simple scalar gradient-scale mismatch does not explain most of the normalisation penalty |
| `0.25 ≤ F < 0.75` | SCALE_EXPLAINS_MEANINGFUL_PART | optimisation/gradient scale explains a meaningful part of the raw-vs-normalised effect |
| `F ≥ 0.75` and `Run014 ≥ Run008 − N` | SCALE_EXPLAINS_MOST | the magnitude-information interpretation is substantially weakened; optimisation scale can account for most of the effect |
| `Run014 < Run008 − N` | BEATS_RAW | report directly; do not rescue the prior hypothesis |
| `Run014 > Run011 + N` | WORSE_THAN_RUN011 | report directly |

Single run, single seed: no statistical significance is claimed. Boundaries within `N` of a
threshold are reported as such.
